## 1. Task definition (locked by the project lead, 2026-10-11)

- **Positive:** an enzyme experimentally reported to degrade a plastic whose backbone is joined by
  carboxylic ester bonds.
- **Hard negative:** an α/β-hydrolase-fold protein (InterPro IPR029058) with the same Ser–His–Asp
  triad, never reported to degrade a plastic.
- **Easy negatives:** wrong-fold ester hydrolases, tRNA-processing hydrolases, and naive controls.
- There is no fold clause in the positive definition: the fold of the positives is reported, not enforced.
- The PHA "generalisation to an unseen lineage" claim is **withdrawn** and must not be reintroduced.

**Wording rules.** Say "candidate nomination", never "discovery". Say the latents "fire at the predicted
catalytic machinery", never that they "prove" mechanism. Present the ablation as a planned causal test, not
a result. Prefer "polyester-degrading enzyme" or "polyester hydrolase" over the loose "plastizyme" in
formal text. Do **not** say other plastics are excluded because they are "hard to break".

**Snippet (definition).**
> We define a positive as an enzyme with an experimental report of degrading a plastic whose backbone is
> joined by carboxylic ester bonds. Negatives are graded by what they share with the positives: hard
> negatives carry the same α/β-hydrolase fold and Ser–His–Asp triad but have no report of plastic
> degradation; easy negatives differ in fold, in catalytic machinery, or in function altogether. A
> negative label therefore means "not reported", not "inactive".

### Why only ester-bonded plastics

- Esters are polar and hydrolysable. The α/β-hydrolase fold and its Ser–His–Asp triad evolved to cut them.
- Amide (polyamide) and urethane (polyurethane) bonds are also hydrolysable but need a different
  catalytic machine. PAZy lists 65 polyamide and 15 polyurethane degraders in our export.
- Polyolefins (PE, PP), polystyrene and PVC have all-carbon backbones with no hydrolysable bond. Reports
  for PE and PP involve oxidative enzymes (laccases, alkane hydroxylases) that modify the chain and do not
  recover monomers.
- Ester bonds are more labile than amide bonds because the amide nitrogen donates into the carbonyl
  (partial double-bond character), making the carbonyl less electrophilic. **[unverified: half-life figures
  not checked against a source; keep the statement qualitative]**
- **Caveat:** bond reactivity is not what limits polyester degradation. PET is hard because of
  crystallinity, rigidity and low water uptake; it degrades much faster near its glass transition
  (about 65–70 °C), which is why engineered enzymes such as LCC are thermostable. **[unverified: Tg
  figure from memory]**

## 3. The negatives (v1.1: 422)

| group | n | what it is | train / val / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| α/β hydrolase hard negatives | 50 | IPR029058, Ser–His–Asp triad, never reported on a plastic | 39 / 1 / 10 |
| Wrong-fold ester hydrolases | 36 | EC 3.1.1, serine nucleophile, other fold (GDSL-SGNH, patatin, β-lactamase) | 23 / 0 / 13 |
| tRNA-processing hydrolases | 99 | EC 3.1.1 by reaction (peptidyl-tRNA hydrolase, D-aminoacyl-tRNA deacylase); His only, no catalytic Ser | 56 / 40 / 3 |
| Naive controls | 237 | kinases, gyrases, oxygenases; not esterases | 166 / 35 / 36 |

- **Naive controls:** assumed not to cut a plastic. This is an inference from what they are, not a measurement.
- **Hard negatives:** same fold, same triad, same carboxylic-ester chemistry; no report of plastic activity.
  "Not reported" is not "inactive". Many have never been assayed on a plastic. Some may be weak degraders.
- **Evidence for that last point:** `P37957` (*B. subtilis* lipase) was a hard negative until it was found
  to be 94% identical to `LipBv`, which PAZy reports as a PET degrader. It was removed from v1.1.
  Consequence: the hard-cell AU-ROC is, if anything, a conservative estimate.
- **What the 'tier 3' label hides:** the legacy `3_fold_matched_esterase` tier has 184 members in v1.1, of
  which only 49 carry the α/β fold. Use `negative_group`, not `class_label`.

### What EC 3.1.1 does (and does not) mean
- EC 3.1.1 = carboxylic ester hydrolases (3 = hydrolases, 3.1 = acting on ester bonds). It is a **reaction
  class, not a fold**. PETase is EC 3.1.1.101.
- It says the enzyme cuts some carboxylic ester, usually a small molecule (p-nitrophenyl esters,
  triglycerides). It does not say the enzyme can attack an insoluble, hydrophobic polymer: that needs
  surface access, a binding cleft that fits a chain, and often thermostability.
- Search gotcha: `ec:3.1.1.*` is a loose prefix match (also catches 3.1.10–3.1.14). Use `ec:3.1.1.-`.
- UniProt counts (reviewed entries, queried 2026-11-25): IPR029058 3,156; with a curated active site
  2,454; IPR029058 and EC 3.1.1 1,172.

**Snippet.**
> A hard negative shares the positives' fold, catalytic triad and ester chemistry, so the model cannot
> separate them by recognising "a serine hydrolase". Because none has been assayed on a plastic, the label
> is a statement about the literature, not about the enzyme.

## 5. Results so far (read only on frozen v1; v1.1 shown for comparison)

v1 scores (max |diff| = 0.0). BLASTp: database = train positives, score = −log10 best e-value, e-value

### How to read these (and what not to claim)
- **Test is spent.** It has been read several times; do not select on these numbers.
- **Differences between rows are noise.** Retraining on +7 positives / −1 hard negative moves AU-ROC by
  about ±0.005, and the hyperparameter cell by about ±0.01, both smaller than the hard-cell interval.
- **The sweep selection is not decisive.** Five cells lie within 0.025 val log-loss; small-C and
  large-eta0 cells changed a lot between v1 and v1.1, so SGD at 5000 epochs on about 600 rows is sensitive
  to data order.
- **BLASTp is below chance on the hard negatives** (AU-ROC 0.43 on v1, 0.46 on v1.1, n=10): a fold-matched
  non-degrader sits about as close to a trained positive as a true degrader does. On wrong-fold and naive
  negatives BLASTp matches or beats the probe, so the probe's advantage is concentrated in the hard cell.
  A paired bootstrap of the difference by group is **not yet run**.
- **The blended number is mostly free weight:** 39 of 62 v1 test negatives are separated perfectly.
- **The decision threshold was tuned where validation had 1 hard negative,** so precision does not transfer
  (v1: precision fell 0.148 from val to test while ranking metrics fell about 0.03–0.04).
- The 9 positives added to v1.1 test are all called by the probe and by BLASTp, but they share components
  with old test proteins and are not independent evidence. LipSh1–3 (755–766 aa, ~0.40 identity to test
  relatives) score only 0.14–0.21 against a threshold of 0.11.

**Snippet.**
> On sequences at most 40% identical to training data, the probe raised AU-PRC from 0.76 (BLASTp) to 0.95
> and recall at 90% precision from 2.4% to 78.6%. The gain is concentrated in the hard cell:
> BLASTp ranks the 10 fold-matched non-degraders no better than chance (AU-ROC 0.43), while the probe
> reaches 0.82 [0.61, 0.96] (component bootstrap, n=10, 7 negative components).

## 6. Explainability (v1; not yet re-run on v1.1)

- Seven SAE latents (InterPLM, ESM-2 650M layer 33): 9529 (Ser), 2661 (Asp), 7734 (His), 7942, 5201, 5255
  (pocket wall), 5271 (C-terminal second shell). Selected on train+val by Cliff's δ ≥ 0.9 and top-10
  stability ≥ 150 of 200 random-half draws, with no catalytic-residue supervision.
- Localisation to curated catalytic residues: **57 of 59 proteins (96.6%)**; 44 of 45 on held-out
  proteins; offsets Ser +1, Asp +3, His +1 (learned on 14). `BPS0016` is a subtilisin and its silence is a
  correct rejection, so 57 of 58 on fold-bearing proteins.
- Nulls: random position 3.2% (41/1,280); **all 10,240 latents through the identical pipeline: median 4.4%,
  none scoring higher** (25 tie latent 9529 on Ser, none passing selection); shuffled position 12.0 / 7.5 /
  6.5%.
- **What it does not show:** the latents fire on 100% of α/β hard negatives, 13.9% of wrong-fold
  hydrolases and 0.0% of tRNA hydrolases. They read out serine-hydrolase machinery. Against 41 fold-matched
  train+val negatives, δ is 0.506–0.716 per protein but 0.116 to −0.178 per component, with cluster-bootstrap
  intervals including zero. Verdict: **undetermined, not refuted.** Correlational only; no ablation done.
- None of the 16 v1.1 additions has a curated catalytic residue, so the 59-protein localisation set does
  not grow.
- **Planned causal test:** zero each selected feature and measure the drop in predicted probability, against
  a matched-control baseline. Not yet done.

## 8. Plan for v1.2 (the cleaner, more powerful dataset)

**Why.** The hard-negative cell is the only part of the benchmark that measures the claim, and it rests on
10 test proteins in 7 components (interval about [0.59, 0.98]). Halving the interval needs about 4× the
components. With 300–400 hard negatives in about 200 components and a 60/20/20 split, test would hold
roughly 60–80 (an estimate, not simulated).

**Sourcing (UniProt reviewed entries).** IPR029058 with a curated active site, never assayed on a plastic,
excluding cutinase and PETase families; take a spread across organisms and subfamilies, not the first 300.

**Risks to control.**
- Label noise: drop any candidate ≥ 90% identical to a positive (the `P37957`/`LipBv` case); flag ≥ 40%.
- No selection by probe score or by distance to the positives.
- Positives remain the other bottleneck: 64% in one component, and 20 positive components in test.

**Split design (one new split, decided after the fetch).** An earlier option was to only swap 9 hard-negative
components from train into val and 9 naive controls back (val 1 → 10 hard negatives, test untouched). That
only redistributes the 50 we have, so v1.2 should use the enlarged pool with one new split. The cost: the
test set changes, so v1 / v1.1 / v1.2 test numbers are no longer directly comparable.

**Planned outputs (for graphs).** Five training runs (seeds 42–46), mean ± SE across seeds, on the **test**
split: ROC table (common FPR grid), PR table with the val-optimal threshold marked (common recall grid),
probability histogram on a log10 scale (clip 1e-8), per-protein score table, summary metrics, per-negative-
group ROC/PR (hard negatives first), and the same for BLASTp. SE is the standard deviation across seeds
divided by √5 and captures training randomness only; component-bootstrap intervals are reported separately.
Validation curves, if shown, are labelled "tuning split, optimistic".

## 9. Methods facts worth keeping

- Embeddings: HuggingFace `EsmModel`, layers 1/9/18/24/30/33, mean and max pooled, CLS/EOS stripped,
  max length 1022. Re-embedding on Apple MPS reproduces stored embeddings exactly.
- Probe selection: C × eta0 grid of 5 × 4 (C 1e-4…1; eta0 1e-4…1e-1), chosen on val log-loss, because val
  AU-PRC saturates and would pick an unconverged corner. C = inverse L2 strength; eta0 = SGD step size.
- Thresholds: val best-F1 for both the probe and BLASTp (carried to test).
- Scripts (committed): `polyester_probe.py`, `polyester_sweep.py`, `polyester_blast.py`,
  `add_pazy_update.py`, `check_pazy_update_uniprot.py`, `build_release.py`, `build_handoff.py`.
- **Reproducibility gap:** the SAE selection, nulls and localisation were produced by uncommitted scripts;
  they must be rewritten and regression-tested on v1 before re-running.
- The metagenomic screen: 30,000 MGnify families; the logs record 165 shortlisted (probe ≥ 0.5; 40 with a
  complete triad). The "450 candidates at ≥ 0.9" figure supplied for the paper is **not in the logs
  [unverified]**; re-score before using it.

## 10. Errors caught during the project (honesty list for the methods or appendix)

- AU-ROC 0.9141 was attributed to the wrong-fold group; it belongs to wrong-fold + α/β combined (n=23). The
  wrong-fold group alone is 0.9890 (n=13).
- A per-protein bootstrap interval was reported on the hard cell; the component bootstrap is wider.
- `BPS0016` was described as a polyamidase; it is a PLA-degrading subtilisin.
- The PHA "unseen lineage" premise was asserted and never tested (14 of 36 share components with trained

- A draft abstract used superseded numbers (172/176, 100 bootstraps) and merged two different nulls.
- The v1 BLASTp score is −log10 e-value; the bitscore does not reproduce the logged numbers.

2. **Test is no longer comparable to v1 / v1.1** (new split). v1 and v1.1 test numbers must not be mixed
   with v1.2 numbers.
3. **Hard negatives outnumber positives** in val and test (112 vs 44, 113 vs 43). Report prevalence and use
   PR metrics with the floor stated.

## 14. Probe architecture and training (for the methods section)

> **KEY METHODS FACT: CLASS WEIGHTING WAS USED.** Every probe was trained with `class_weight="balanced"`
> (positives weighted 1.24, negatives 0.84), so the loss is class-weighted. No weighting by negative group (hard
> negatives) or by homology family was used. See 14.2. This must be stated in the paper's methods section.


**Model.** A linear probe on frozen ESM-2 650M embeddings. Only a weight vector (1,280 numbers) and a bias are trained
(1,281 parameters); ESM-2 is never fine-tuned.

**Pipeline.** Sequence (truncated at 1,022 residues) -> ESM-2 650M (`facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`, HuggingFace
`EsmModel`, Apple MPS) -> layer-33 hidden states, one 1,280-d vector per residue with the CLS and end tokens removed ->
**max-pool over residues** (largest value per dimension) -> per-dimension standardisation (`StandardScaler` fitted on
the train split only) -> linear layer z = w.x + b -> sigmoid p = 1 / (1 + exp(-z)) -> positive if p >= threshold.

**Objective.** Class-weighted log-loss (cross-entropy) with an L2 penalty on w. In scikit-learn's SGD form the
penalty strength is alpha = 1 / (C * n_train); with C = 0.01 and n_train = 789, alpha is about 0.127. Class weights are
"balanced" (n / (2 * n_class)): positives are 40% of training rows and are up-weighted accordingly.

**Optimiser.** `SGDClassifier(loss="log_loss", penalty="l2", learning_rate="constant", eta0=1e-4, max_iter=5000,
tol=None, class_weight="balanced", random_state=seed)`: one-sample stochastic gradient descent, constant step,
no momentum, no early stopping, a fixed 5,000 passes over the training data. The seed changes only the shuffling order.

**Selection.** C in {1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1, 1} x eta0 in {1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1}, chosen by the lowest validation
log-loss (AU-PRC saturates and would pick an unconverged corner). Selected on v1.2: C = 0.01, eta0 = 0.0001 (the same
cell as v1). The feature set (max-pooled layer 33) was chosen in v1 from mean and max pooling at layers 1, 9, 18, 24,
30 and 33 and was not re-swept on v1.2.

**Training runs and threshold.** Five runs (seeds 42-46), train split only. For every run and every threshold from
0.001 to 0.999, precision, recall, FPR and F1 are computed on validation and averaged over the runs; the single
threshold with the best mean validation F1 (**0.15**) is then frozen and applied to test. The probabilities are not
calibrated (balanced weighting inflates them), which is why the threshold is low.

**Baseline.** BLASTp: database of the train positives, query = each val/test protein, score = -log10 of the best-hit
e-value (no hit = e-value 1e9); the e-value cut-off is chosen on validation by F1 over a half-decade grid.

**Code.** `embed_esm2.py`, `polyester_sweep.py`, `polyester_probe.py`, `polyester_phase1_v12.py`,
`polyester_blast.py`, `blast_phase1_v12.py`. Libraries: transformers, scikit-learn (StandardScaler, SGDClassifier,
log_loss, roc_auc_score, average_precision_score, precision_recall_curve, roc_curve), Biopython (PairwiseAligner),
mmseqs2, BLAST+.

**Design choice.** The backbone is frozen by design: the project asks what ESM-2 already encodes, so only a probe
is trained and the model is never fine-tuned. The probe is linear, which tests whether the information is linearly
decodable and keeps the weights inspectable for the SAE analysis. A non-linear (MLP) head on the frozen embeddings was
not tried on v1.2 and remains a possible comparison.

**Snippet.**
> We train a linear probe on frozen ESM-2 (650M) embeddings. Each protein is represented by the element-wise
> maximum of its layer-33 residue embeddings (1,280 dimensions), standardised with training-set statistics, and
> classified by L2-regularised, class-balanced logistic regression trained by stochastic gradient descent
> (5,000 epochs, constant step 1e-4, C = 0.01). The decision threshold (0.15) maximises the mean validation F1 over
> five training runs.

### 14.1 Loss function (binary cross-entropy)

**Per-protein loss.** Binary cross-entropy, called log-loss in scikit-learn (`loss="log_loss"`): for label y in {0, 1}
(1 = polyester degrader) and predicted probability p = sigmoid(w.x + b),
loss = -[ y ln p + (1 - y) ln(1 - p) ]. It punishes confident mistakes heavily (y = 1, p = 0.01 costs 4.6; p = 0.99
costs 0.01) and is convex in (w, b).

**Training objective.** (1/n) sum_i weight_i x loss_i + (alpha / 2) ||w||^2, with alpha = 1 / (C n_train) = 0.127
(C = 0.01, n = 789). `class_weight="balanced"` gives weight n / (2 n_class): 789 / (2 x 317) = 1.24 for positives and
789 / (2 x 472) = 0.84 for negatives, so the classes count equally although positives are 40% of training rows. The
bias is not penalised. Minimised by constant-step SGD (eta0 = 1e-4), one protein per update, 5,000 passes.

**Validation log-loss used for selection.** The plain, unweighted cross-entropy of the output probabilities on the
validation proteins (`sklearn.metrics.log_loss`, no class weights): 0.2228 for the selected cell. Reference points on
validation: predicting the prevalence (0.186) for every protein gives about 0.48; random guessing at 0.5 gives 0.693.
Balanced weighting inflates the probabilities relative to the true prevalence, so they are not calibrated and the best
decision threshold is 0.15, not 0.5. Log-loss was used only to rank hyperparameter cells, never reported as a result.

**Snippet.**
> The probe is trained by minimising class-balanced binary cross-entropy with an L2 penalty on the weights,
> using stochastic gradient descent. Hyperparameters were chosen by the unweighted validation cross-entropy.

### 14.2 Class weighting (important; state in the methods and in the limitations)

**What was done.** Class weighting on the positive/negative label: scikit-learn `class_weight="balanced"`, weight =
n / (2 x n_class). On the v1.2 train split (n = 789: 317 positives, 472 negatives) positives weigh 1.24 and negatives
0.84, so the two classes contribute equally to the loss although positives are 40% of training rows. Used in every
training run, in the C x eta0 sweep, and in all five final runs. (v1 and v1.1 probes used the same setting.)

**What was not done.**
- No weighting by negative group: each of the 225 training hard negatives has the same weight as any other negative.
- No weighting by homology family: 82% of the train positives belong to one component (`BPS0190`, 259 enzymes) and each
  carries the same weight as any other positive.
- The weighting was not swept (no comparison with unweighted or other weights).

**Consequences to report.**
1. The output probabilities are inflated relative to the true prevalence (about 0.2 in validation and test), so they
   are not calibrated; the F1-optimal threshold is 0.15 and not 0.5. Thresholds are not comparable with an
   unweighted probe.
2. Training uses the weighted loss; hyperparameter selection uses the unweighted validation cross-entropy (14.1).
3. AU-ROC and AU-PRC are ranking metrics and do not depend on the threshold, but the weighting changed the fitted
   weights and therefore the ranking. A weighting sweep (none, balanced, hard-negative up-weight, one vote per family)
   is on the list of sweeps not yet done (16.7).
4. BLASTp has no equivalent training step.

**Snippet.**
> The probe was trained with class-balanced weights (positives 1.24, negatives 0.84 for 317 positives and 472
> negatives), so that both classes contribute equally to the loss. Hard negatives and protein families were not
> weighted separately. As a result, predicted probabilities are not calibrated to the dataset prevalence, and the
> decision threshold (0.15) was chosen on validation.

### 15.2 Decision threshold, chosen by mean validation F1
- **Grid tried (103 probability thresholds):** 0.001, 0.005, 0.01 to 0.99 in steps of 0.01, 0.995, 0.999.
- **Rule:** for each of the five runs compute F1 on validation at every threshold, average over the runs, and take the
  threshold with the highest mean F1. One threshold for all runs.
- **Selected: 0.15** (mean validation F1 0.8128; precision 0.816, recall 0.809). Neighbours: 0.10 -> 0.783, 0.12 ->
  0.787, 0.14 -> 0.804, 0.16 -> 0.791, 0.18 -> 0.771, 0.20 -> 0.750. The curve has a broad shoulder from 0.10 to 0.16
  and fluctuates by about 0.02 between neighbours (44 validation positives), so 0.15 is a local best and not a sharp
  optimum.
- **At 0.15 on test (frozen):** precision 0.727, recall 0.744, F1 0.736.
- **Precision-target thresholds** (95 / 90 / 85 / 80%): the lowest grid threshold whose mean validation precision
  reaches the target: 0.44, 0.40, 0.32, 0.15; carried unchanged to test.

### 15.3 BLASTp e-value cut-off, chosen by validation F1
- **Grid tried:** half-decade e-values from 10 down to 1e-30.
- **Rule:** the cut-off with the best validation F1 (hit score >= -log10 e). **Selected:** e <= 3.2e-6 (validation
  precision 0.623, recall 0.750, F1 0.680).
- **Why:** it is the same rule as the probe's threshold, so the two methods get the same chance to adapt to validation.

### 15.4 Caveat
Validation therefore gives optimistic numbers: it chose C, eta0 and the thresholds. Test AU-ROC and AU-PRC do not
depend on the thresholds, but the precision, recall and F1 on test do depend on them.

## 16. Interpretation notes for the discussion section (2026-11-25)

**16.1 What a shortcut-only probe would look like.** If the probe read only "bacterial or not", the matched cell
(bacterial positives against bacterial hard negatives, where domain-only scores 0.500) would give AU-ROC about 0.50
with an interval around 0.5, AU-PRC at its floor (0.51), bacterial hard negatives scored as high as the positives,
and a large gap between eukaryotic negatives (near perfect) and bacterial ones (chance). Pre-set criteria:
failure = matched-cell interval includes 0.5 or AU-PRC within about 0.05 of the floor; success = matched cell close to
the eukaryotic cell (about 0.9 or higher) and clearly above the baselines. Observed: AU-ROC 0.757 [0.554, 0.925],
AU-PRC 0.826 against a floor of 0.51; eukaryotic cell 0.952. Verdict: partial; some signal beyond domain, imprecise.
Not yet checked: score distributions of bacterial hard negatives; an Actinomycetota-only cell (positives are mostly
Actinomycetota); a probe trained on bacterial rows only. The probe was trained on all train rows, so the shortcut
was present in training; the matched cell removes it only at evaluation.

**16.2 Wording.** "In a taxonomy-controlled cell the probe retains above-chance discrimination (AU-ROC 0.76, 95% CI
0.55 to 0.93; AU-PRC 0.83 against a floor of 0.51), so its signal is not solely taxonomic. The estimate is imprecise
(23 positives, 22 negatives) and may reflect subfamily similarity to known degraders rather than polyester specificity."
Avoid "the probe learns an enzyme signal".

**16.3 Two kinds of standard error.** (1) Seed SE: sd over five training runs / sqrt(5); training randomness only;
under 0.001 here; BLASTp is deterministic and has none. (2) Family-resampling SE: spread of the score when homology
components are resampled; test sampling; 0.017-0.025 for the probe's AU-ROC and 0.05-0.06 for BLASTp's. The paper's
standard errors are of the first kind and must be labelled so; the second is what bounds the claim.

**16.4 A reviewer's view of probe versus BLASTp.** The probe is ahead on AU-ROC and AU-PRC on validation and test and
at every precision target (test recall 0.349 / 0.419 / 0.488 at 95 / 90 / 85% precision against BLASTp 0.186), and the
paired all-negatives difference excludes zero (13.7). Not "every cell": on wrong-fold (5 test negatives) and
tRNA-processing (3) BLASTp scores as high (0.914 against 0.870; 0.992 against 0.969 AU-ROC). BLASTp is a weak baseline
(it sees only positives and uses one best hit): add nearest-neighbour with positives and negatives, a profile HMM, or a
k-mer classifier. The largest gaps sit in cells full of eukaryotic negatives (taxonomy shortcut). Recall at high
precision is modest (35-49%). Test was read several times. The paired test on the hard cells is not done.

**16.5 Cliff's delta.** Conventional bands (Romano et al. 2006, citation unverified): |d| < 0.147 negligible, < 0.33
small, < 0.474 medium, otherwise large. They are descriptive labels, not a selection rule: the v1 latent selection
(d >= 0.9 plus top-10 stability in at least 150 of 200 half-draws) was pre-set and is kept; changing it to 0.147
would admit hundreds of the 10,240 latents and be a post-hoc change. Report d with a component-bootstrap interval, the
band as a label, and its place in the random-latent null. v1: per-component d against fold-matched negatives
0.116 to -0.178 (negligible to small, intervals including 0). v1.2 gives 337 hard negatives in train+val (v1 had
41), so the test has real power, but taxonomy must be controlled for latents too.

**16.6 SAE and ablation, caveats.** Catalytic latents fired on 100% of alpha/beta hard negatives in v1: they read out
serine-hydrolase machinery, so firing alone does not separate polyester degraders from hard negatives. An ablation
(zero the selected latents, measure the change in probe score) shows dependence, not mechanism; outcomes: equal drop
for positives and hard negatives -> generic hydrolase signal; matched-cell discrimination collapses -> the latents
carry the separating signal; scores barely move -> the probe does not depend on them (the likeliest, since v1's best
single latent had d 0.847 and the margin looked distributed). Controls: matched random latents, a reconstruction-only
baseline, the matched bacterial cell. The v1 SAE selection, null and localisation scripts were never committed and
must be rewritten and regression-tested on v1 (57 of 59, 4.4% null) before running on v1.2.

**16.7 Hyperparameters that could still be swept.** Cheap: component weighting (82% of train positives are one
family), hard-negative or group weights, a layer and pooling re-sweep (mean, max, mean+max at layers 1, 9, 18, 24, 30,
33) on v1.2, an L-BFGS solver (no learning rate or seed), epochs. Larger: L1 or elastic-net penalty, an MLP head on the
frozen embeddings, attention or top-k pooling, backbone size (8M, 3B, 15B, frozen), SAE features as input; and the
threshold rule (F1, precision target, Youden's J). Cautions: keep the grid small (validation has 44 positives, so
every cell raises selection optimism), choose by validation log-loss, fix the grid before looking, read test once,
judge each change on the matched bacterial cell.

## 17. Glossary (for the methods section)

- **C:** inverse L2 regularisation strength. Penalty alpha = 1 / (C x n_train). Small C = strong shrinkage (may
  underfit), large C = weak shrinkage (may overfit). Selected 0.01.
- **eta0:** SGD step size (learning rate). Too large oscillates, too small does not converge in 5,000 epochs.
  Selected 0.0001. It interacts with C.
- **Threshold:** probability cut-off (0.15); probabilities are not calibrated because of balanced class weights.
- **Prevalence floor:** the positive fraction of the evaluated cell; the AU-PRC of random ranking.
- **Hard negative:** alpha/beta-hydrolase-fold, Ser-His-Asp protein with no report of degrading a plastic
  ("not reported", not "inactive").
- **Matched cell:** bacterial positives against bacterial hard negatives (domain carries no information).
- **EC 3.1.1.-:** carboxylic ester hydrolases, a reaction class and not a fold; PETase is EC 3.1.1.101.
- **Homology component:** group of sequences linked at 40% global identity; whole components never cross splits;
  every interval resamples components.

## 18. How the seven SAE latents were identified in v1 (reconstructed and verified 2026-11-26)

The original selection script was never committed. It was reconstructed from the stored arrays and **reproduces the
stored v1 result exactly**: Cliff's delta to 6e-8 and the top-10 hit counts identically for all seven latents
(`src/biopet_sae/sae_select_latents.py`, outputs and stored arrays in `paper/v1_sae_selection/`). Localisation to
catalytic residues (offsets, 57 of 59, null) is a later stage and is not covered here.

**18.1 Activations.** The InterPLM SAE (`Elana/InterPLM-esm2-650m`, `ae_normalized.pt`, 10,240 latents, input 1,280-d)
is applied to the ESM-2 650M layer-33 hidden state of every residue (SAE reconstruction cosine 0.898; about 1.4% of
latents active per residue; about 6,260 latents active somewhere in an average protein). Each protein is then
summarised by the **maximum over residues** of each latent (`feat_max`, 847 x 10,240 in v1); a mean-pooled version
(`feat_mean`) was also saved but **was not used**: recomputing delta from `feat_mean` differs from the stored values
by up to 0.62, from `feat_max` by 6e-8. Max-pooling keeps the strongest residue-level response, which suits a
catalytic signal confined to a few residues.

**18.2 Cliff's delta, per latent, over every positive-negative pair.** Rows: train and validation proteins only
(v1: 346 positives, 361 negatives = 707 proteins). For each latent, over all 346 x 361 = **124,906 pairs**:
delta = [#pairs with positive > negative - #pairs with positive < negative] / #pairs, from -1 to +1; tied pairs
(common, because most latents are zero in most proteins) count for neither. Computed from ranks (delta = 2U / (n_pos
n_neg) - 1, the Mann-Whitney U), which equals the pair count. Example: latent 9529, delta 0.959: the positive protein
has the larger activation in about 98% of pairs (ties split). All 10,240 latents were scored; **26 have delta >= 0.9**
(maximum 0.959).

**18.3 The 200 resamples are random half-draws, not 200 proteins with replacement.** Each of 200 draws picks a random
half of the 707 proteins (353) **without replacement** (`numpy` generator, seed 0), recomputes delta for all 10,240
latents, and records the 10 latents with the largest delta. `hits` = the number of draws (of 200) in which a latent is
in the top 10. This reproduces the stored counts exactly. A with-replacement draw of 200 proteins would give different
(lower) counts (my contrast run: 9529 -> 197, 7942 -> 146, 5201 -> 184, 5255 -> 132, 2661 -> 115, 7734 -> 118, 5271 ->
109), so it is not what was done.

**18.4 Selection rule.** delta >= 0.9 **and** hits >= 150 of 200 (75%). Of the 26 latents with delta >= 0.9,
**7 survive**:

| latent | delta | top-10 in draws |
|---|---|---|
| 9529 | 0.9589 | 200 / 200 |
| 5201 | 0.9494 | 200 |
| 7942 | 0.9370 | 189 |
| 5255 | 0.9360 | 176 |
| 2661 | 0.9360 | 174 |
| 5271 | 0.9340 | 157 |
| 7734 | 0.9338 | 159 |

Thirty latents were in a top-10 at least once. Nineteen latents with delta >= 0.9 failed the stability test, for
example 5965 (delta 0.934, 139 draws), 2804 (0.930, 109), 2473 (0.928, 101), 8554 (0.928, 78).

**18.5 Reading it honestly.**
- The top-10 criterion is a competition: many latents have almost identical delta (0.93-0.96), so who stays in the
  top 10 depends on small rank differences. Latent 5965 (delta 0.934) is as separating as 5271 and 7734 (0.934) but
  misses the cut by 11 draws; the cut-off of 150 is a convention, so the seven are not uniquely "the" catalytic
  latents.
- The stability is of delta against a negative set that is 88% not fold-matched (v1 train+val: 361 negatives, 41 of them
  alpha/beta). Against the 41 fold-matched negatives alone, none of the 10,240 latents reaches delta 0.9 (maximum
  0.847) and the seven fall to 0 of 200 draws (v1 section 22).
- The delta values are stable; the hit counts depend on the draw (a second run with other draws gave 70% and 73% for
  7734 and 5271 where the stored run gave 79.5% and 78.5%).

**18.6 For v1.2.** Train+val now holds 1,026 proteins including 337 hard negatives, so the same procedure can be run
against fold-matched negatives with real power. SAE features have so far been extracted for the 847 v1 proteins only;
the 16 + 400 new sequences still need a forward pass through ESM-2 and the SAE before the script can run on v1.2
(`sae_select_latents.py` stops with a clear message otherwise). Taxonomy has to be controlled here too (latents can
encode domain).

## 20. Catalytic-triad annotation with three SAE latents: final rule and results (2026-11-26)

> **Update:** for aspartate, latent 157 was replaced by a nine-latent classifier (20.12). The final test figures are in 20.12 and section 21; aspartate figures in 20.4, 20.5 and 20.9 are the earlier single-latent version.

Files: `paper/v1_2_triad/` (activations summary `windows.npz`, `final_exact.json`, `precision_recall.json`, earlier selection rounds). Code: `positive_triad_sites.py`, `triad_latent_search.py` (activations), `triad_exact_final.py` (final), `triad_hits.py`, `triad_annotate.py`, `asp_search.py`, `triad_window_search.py`.

### 20.1 Question and design
Detection (is it a polyester degrader?) and annotation (where is its catalytic triad?) are separate. Annotation needs no negative class: it uses every alpha/beta hydrolase with an annotated Ser, Asp and His, degraders and non-degraders together, because they share the machinery. Hard negatives are therefore an answer key, not a problem. A located triad is **not** evidence of plastic degradation.

**Proteins.** Choose on train+val: 340 (15 degraders); held out: test 105 (3 degraders). 88 homology components in train+val, 47 in test; no test protein is >= 40% identical to a train/val protein. Only 18 of 404 degraders have usable annotations (6 families).

### 20.2 Ground truth: how reliable is it?
UniProt "Active site" features. Evidence tags on the annotations of 426 hard negatives (1327 tags; degraders not tallied):

| evidence code | meaning | tags | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECO:0000250 | inferred from sequence similarity | 780 | 58.8% |

### 20.3 The method (final rule)
1. ESM-2 650M layer 33, per residue, through the InterPLM SAE: a (length x 10,240) table.
2. A latent is **correct** on a protein if its maximum activation is >= 0.3 **and the residue where that maximum occurs is exactly the annotated catalytic residue**.
3. For each residue type, count correct proteins over train+val for all 10,240 latents; choose the latent with the most (tie: higher mean peak activation).
4. 1,000 random halves of train+val without replacement (seed 0): how often each chosen latent is first.
5. Apply the three latents unchanged to test.

Threshold 0.3 is a convention carried from v1, not derived.

- Widths +/-3, +/-1 and exact give the same three latents and the same numbers under the naming rule on train+val (the chosen latents peak on the residue when correct).
- Only 3 held-out degraders. The latents also fire on non-degraders. Scope: alpha/beta hydrolases only.

## 21. The pipeline: nomination plus active-site annotation (built 2026-11-26)

Code `src/biopet_sae/plastizyme_pipeline.py` (predict, and `--check`) and `train_pipeline_probe.py`; saved models `models/pipeline_v1_2/` (`probe.npz`: scaler, five weight vectors, thresholds; `triad.json`: latents and aspartate classifier).

## 24. How the v1.2 split was made (algorithm, for the methods section)

Code: `src/biopet_sae/build_v12.py` (pair identities from `select_hard_negatives_v12.py`).

**In one sentence:** similar proteins are grouped into families, and whole families are dealt into train, validation and
test so that each split gets a fair mix.

```
SPLIT(proteins)
1. Related pairs: mmseqs2 all-vs-all; every pair with local identity >= 30% is re-aligned globally
   (BLOSUM62, gap -11/-1, free end gaps; identity over the shorter sequence).
2. Families: link any two proteins with identity >= 40%; linked chains form one family (466 families).
3. Set a target count of each protein type for train, val and test.
4. Repeat 20,000 times (random seed 42):
       order the families, larger ones tending to come first (random jitter)
       for each family:
           put the WHOLE family into the split where it most reduces the remaining gap to the targets
       score = sum over (split, type) of |achieved - target| / total of that type
5. Keep the lowest-scoring assignment.
6. Check: no two proteins in different splits have identity >= 40%.
```

**Targets (train / val / test):** PET degraders 79 / 10.5 / 10.5; other polyester degraders 60 / 20 / 20; hard negatives
50 / 25 / 25; other negatives 70 / 15 / 15.

**Step 4 explained.** Each attempt places families one at a time, whole, where they fill the biggest remaining gap;
gaps are weighted by the size of each protein type, so a small group is not ignored beside a large one. Many random
orders are tried because this is simple and close enough; it does not guarantee the best possible split. The seed is
fixed, so the split is reproducible.

**Result:** train 789 (64.4%), val 237 (19.3%), test 200 (16.3%).

| split | positives | hard negatives | other negatives | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 317 (40.2%) | 225 (28.5%) | 247 (31.3%) | 789 |
| val | 44 (18.6%) | 112 (47.3%) | 81 (34.2%) | 237 |
| test | 43 (21.5%) | 113 (56.5%) | 44 (22.0%) | 200 |

An even 60/20/20 is not possible: one family holds 259 of the 404 positives and cannot be divided.

**Checks:** maximum cross-split identity 0.398; a second method (mmseqs2 clustering at 40% local identity) flags 2 of 506
clusters spanning splits, with global identities 0.386-0.391; no protein in two splits; no label changed.
**Caveat:** pairs under 30% local identity in step 1 were not re-aligned, on the assumption that none reaches 40% globally.

> Sequences sharing at least 40% global identity were grouped into families, and whole families were assigned to
> training, validation or test, so that no two proteins in different splits exceeded 40% identity (observed maximum
> 39.8%).
