BioPET-SAE Dataset v1

A homology-bounded benchmark for detecting polyester-degrading enzymes (plastizymes) from protein sequence, with the negative set classified by fold rather than by EC class. 847 proteins — 811 modelled, 36 held out. Every number on this page was re-derived from the released file.

Read this first

Two properties bound every claim you can make with this dataset. They are not defects to work around — they are the actual difficulty of the problem.

51
genuinely fold-matched negatives in the whole dataset, and 1 of them is in validation
66%
of positives sit in one homology component — BPS0190, 256 of 388

A classifier that separates plastizymes from random proteins has learned "is this a hydrolase". Only the α/β-hydrolase-fold negatives test the real question, and there are 51 of them: 40 train, 1 val, 10 test.

Bootstrap over component, never over proteins. With two thirds of the positives in a single family, a per-protein confidence interval counts that family 256 times. It is wrong, not merely optimistic. In our own run, the per-protein Cliff's δ of 0.72 for the strongest SAE latent fell to 0.12 and its interval spanned zero once resampling respected homology.

Together these are why the headline test AU-ROC of 0.9681 becomes 0.8167 against fold-matched negatives alone, with a 95% CI reaching 0.676.

Why tRNA-processing is separated out

99 negatives are D-aminoacyl-tRNA deacylase (EC 3.1.1.96, PF02580, n=56) and peptidyl-tRNA hydrolase (EC 3.1.1.29, PF01195, n=43). They match on EC reaction class — carboxylic-ester hydrolase — but share neither the fold nor the catalytic machinery. Of the 43 carrying a curated active site, every one is a single histidine "proton acceptor"; the other 56 have no active-site annotation at all. Not one has an annotated serine nucleophile. They are trivially separable and inflate any metric computed against them. They are kept, and labelled, so you can exclude them deliberately.

Why wrong-fold EC 3.1.1 is separated out

36 negatives are ester hydrolases with a serine nucleophile in a different fold — GDSL/SGNH (PF13472, PF00657), patatin (PF01734), β-lactamase-fold (PF00144). Intermediate difficulty, and a useful control for whether a method detects a serine or a fold.

Maximum cross-split sequence identity: train/val 0.3967, train/test 0.3800, val/test 0.3983. No cross-split pair exceeds 40%.

Validation is not a miniature of test. Its 76 negatives are 40 tRNA hydrolases, 35 naive controls and 1 genuine esterase. The 99 tRNA entries form only three homology components, and identity-bounded splitting keeps a component intact — so one component of 40 went wholly to val. Tuning on val is tuning against the easy task. In our run this is why the threshold carried from val gave test precision of 0.830. If you tune on val, say so, and report test decomposed by negative_group.

How to report results on this dataset

  1. Report test decomposed by negative_group. A single headline number is not interpretable here.
  2. Bootstrap over component, not over proteins. With 66% of positives in one component, per-protein intervals are invalid.
  3. Say whether you tuned on validation, and remember it holds 1 fold-matched negative.
  4. State n for the fold-matched cell. It is 10 in test. Any claim there is low-powered, and should carry an interval.
  5. Leave heldout_pha alone until you want a generalisation number.

Known limitations

  • 51 fold-matched negatives is too few, and the val allocation of 1 makes val unusable for the hard task. Expanding this is the highest-value fix: UniProt reviewed, IPR029058 plus three curated ACT_SITE residues, never assayed on a plastic, excluding the cutinase and PETase families. Thousands are available; 300–400 would make every split usable.
  • Positive homology density limits power independently of the negative set, and no number of new negatives fixes it. Needs one-vote-per-family weighting, or genuine positive diversity beyond the dominant cutinase clade.
  • Negative labels are assumptions. No negative was assayed for polyester degradation; some may be weak degraders. PEZy-miner's 36 assayed non-degraders are not yet incorporated and would be a genuine measured-negative set.
  • 48 of 388 positives are unverified, 29 of those extrapolated-only.
  • Tier assignment used EC codes. A rebuild on InterPro/Pfam fold is the right design; negative_group approximates it post hoc.

Provenance

Positives from PAZy and PlasticDB. 118 candidates were excluded during assembly: 109 for a non-ester mechanism (polyamide, polyurethane, polyolefin, rubber, polycarbonate, polyether), 8 for the wrong fold (proteases degrading PLA, chitinases), and 1 manually — BPS0023, named a PETase by homology but only ever assayed on PCL.

Negatives from UniProt queries in src/biopet_sae/fetch_negatives.py, with a per-phylum quota matched to the positives (the PET class is 72.6% Actinomycetota) and a filter removing anything resembling a known plastizyme. The quota could not be met for the esterase tier — UniProt holds only 174 reviewed Actinomycetota EC 3.1.1 entries against a quota of 177 — which is how the tRNA-processing hydrolases entered the set.

Annotation in this release from the UniProt REST API, fields ec, xref_pfam, xref_interpro and ft_act_site, fetched 2026-10-11 and cached at data/processed/uniprot_negative_annotation.json.
