ESM-2 650M · layer 33

Catalytic Latent Atlas for Plastizymes

Seven SAE latents were selected on train + validation only, by a rank statistic, with no structural information and no look at where they fire. They were then frozen and localised. Three fire in a catalytic-residue neighbourhood — not on the residue itself, but a fixed one to three positions before it. That offset was learned on train+val, then applied unchanged to test and to external proteins. Scored against every protein with a curated UniProt active site — never an estimate or a motif proxy.

Materials and methods — how the latents were found

1 · Representation

Each sequence passes through ESM-2 650M; layer-33 residue embeddings (1,280-d) go into the InterPLM sparse autoencoder, giving 10,240 latents per residue. Each latent is max-pooled over residues, so every protein becomes one 10,240-vector.

2 · Selection statistic — Cliff's delta

For each latent, δ = 2·AUC − 1 between polyester degraders and non-degraders. It is rank-based, so it never divides by a variance — which matters because 1,104 latents are exactly zero across all negatives and would blow up a mean-difference statistic. Ties push δ toward zero, so a latent firing in <5% of positives cannot score highly (279 such latents; max δ 0.04, none in the top 100). No threshold parameter is needed.

3 · Split discipline

δ was computed on train + validation only (707 sequences, 346 positives). The test split took no part in selection, and no structural or active-site information was used at any point.

4 · Stability — 200-draw bootstrap, and the cutoff

Selection was repeated on 200 random halves of train+val, recomputing δ each time and recording how often each latent stayed in the top 10. A latent surviving most resamples is a finding; one appearing in a single fit is not.

The criterion is top-10 in ≥75% of draws (≥150/200) and δ ≥ 0.9. Two notes on why. First, the δ floor is not what selects — 26 latents clear δ 0.9 but only 7 clear the frequency bar, so the bootstrap does all the work. Second, 75% sits in a gap: thresholds anywhere from 70% to 78% return the identical seven, because the next latent down (5965, 139/200) is 18 draws below the seventh (5271, 157/200). An 80% cutoff would instead have excluded 7734 by a single draw (159/200). The gap argument is suggestive rather than decisive — 18 draws at 75% against 15 at 80% — but the one-draw exclusion is the clearer objection.

5 · What survived

The ordering is not resolvable. The gap between rank 1 and rank 10 (0.031) is smaller than the median bootstrap CI width (0.067), so this is a stable set of latents, not a ranking.

This is the complete list of latents meeting the criterion — every latent with δ ≥ 0.9 that reached the top 10 in at least 150 of 200 draws. The cutoff row and the first excluded latent are shown so the boundary is visible rather than implied.

6 · Freeze, then localise — the neighbourhood protocol

These latents do not fire on the catalytic residue. They peak one to three positions before it, and by the residue itself the activation has fallen to a median 0.24 (Ser), 0.21 (Asp) and 0.43 (His) of the peak, across all 59 proteins. It is above half in 0/59, 0/58 and 13/59 of them. The mapping is indirect, so it is scored that way:

  1. The seven latents were fixed. Nothing below fed back into selection.
  2. The residue with the highest activation is the latent's peak.
  3. The offset from peak to catalytic residue was learned on train+val only: Ser +1 (13/14), Asp +3 (9/14), His +1 (12/14). Those offsets were then applied unchanged.
  4. Exact — the learned offset lands on the curated residue.
  5. Recovered — it does not, so the window slides ±3 to the nearest residue of the right type (S / D-E / H) that still carries non-zero activation. Counted and reported separately, never merged into "exact".
  6. Miss — no such residue in the window.

The unit is the protein, not the residue. Within a protein the three latents agree 100% of the time — either all three residues are located or none is, because a canonical α/β-hydrolase active site either is present or it is not. Counting 176 residue-level outcomes would treat one failure as three, so results are reported per protein: 57 of 59 fully located, with the residue breakdown (172/176) shown beneath. The two failures are BPS0016, a polyamidase and the only non-α/β-hydrolase in the set, and Q9Y7G8.

Null control. Applying the same ±3 rule from a random position lands on the curated residue in 3.2% of trials (41/1,280). Widening to ±5 recovers nothing further, so the window is correcting one-residue offsets rather than searching until something is found.

Four of the seven had no curated target to score against. Mapped onto solved structures, three (5201, 7942, 5255) peak a median 8–10 Å from the catalytic serine (range 8–25 Å) while a median of 20–75 residues away in sequence (range 11–128): the substrate pocket, not catalytic residues. The fourth (5271) sits further out — median 14.1 Å (range 9.4–20.0) and a median 88 residues away, in the C-terminal third of the chain (mean relative position 0.89), peaking one residue after a tyrosine in 33 of 59 proteins. Second shell, not pocket-lining.

8 · The null that matters — random features

Every one of the 10,240 latents went through the identical pipeline: peak → offset learned on train+val → applied to the 45 held-out entries → exact or ±3 recovered. Each random latent gets its own fitted offset and is held to a looser rule than the real ones (no activation requirement).

A typical latent localises at 4.4%. The floor is not zero because a wrong position plus a ±3 window often lands on some serine: latent 77, whose gaps across train+val run from −125 to +67 and whose rule is a mode seen 4 times in 14, still scores 58% on held-out proteins. Measuring that floor is what makes 97.7% mean something.

Shuffled-position null (real latents, protein A's peak used on protein B, 200 permutations): Ser 12.0%, Asp 7.5%, His 6.5% — against 97.8–100% real.

They are not unique. 26 latents tie with 9529 on Ser, 8 with 2661, 9 with 7734; ten of the 26 correlate above 0.8 with 9529 and are redundant copies. But none of the ties passes selection — the best reaches 0.695 on the bootstrap against a 0.75 cutoff. They localise without separating the classes, which is what the two-stage filter exists to catch. The supportable claim is a percentile, not "the serine detector".

The 38/38 uses a sequence motif rather than an experimental annotation, so it is weaker than its size suggests — it confirms the peak sits on a nucleophile elbow, not that the serine is catalytically active. Still, 7 of the 38 use an elbow variant that appears in no train+val positive (GISLG, GNSVG, GFSLG, GHSFG, GCSLG, GLSLG), so the latent is locating a structural position, not matching a memorised string.

The uncomfortable number: experimental ground truth on held-out plastizymes is 2 sequences. Everything larger is either not held out (18), motif-based (38), or the wrong organisms. The 46 curated cutinases are the best compromise — experimental, outside this dataset, right family — but they are curated cutinases, not enzymes assayed on plastic.

Caveat

The seven latents correlate at 0.88 on average and 90% of their variance is a single axis — "how canonical is this active site". (Computed over the 474 polyester positives plus genuine, non-tRNA tier-3 esterases; six latents give 0.881 / 90.1% on that basis and seven give 0.882 / 89.9%, so adding 5271 changes nothing.) Against genuine esterases, which share the same triad, they reach only 0.79 AU-ROC on test. These features explain catalytic machinery, not plastic specificity.
