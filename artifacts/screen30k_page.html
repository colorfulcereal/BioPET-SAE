Metagenomic Plastizyme Screen

The polyester-degradation probe — ESM-2 650M embeddings, trained on PAZy/PlasticDB positives against fold-matched esterases — was run over unannotated predicted proteins from three MGnify metagenome studies. Sequences were filtered to α/β-hydrolase candidates carrying a nucleophile-elbow motif, collapsed to one representative per 90%-identity family, and sampled. Each candidate is reported with its probe score and the three SAE catalytic latents that the mechanistic half of this project identified, so a nomination can be read as "scored high and the triad is where the latents say it should be" rather than a bare number. Nothing here has been tested in a laboratory.

Provenance and curation funnel — studies, biomes, filters, family collapse

Source studies

Predicted CDS were pulled from MGnify assembly analyses. Nothing here is from UniProt or from any curated plastizyme database — the point of the screen is sequences that no one has annotated.

Curation funnel

Each step is a hard filter applied in order. The counts below are the population surviving that step.

Family collapse

One sequence was kept per 90%-identity family. Metagenome assemblies are redundant — the same enzyme recurs across samples, contigs and near-identical strains — so without collapsing, a single abundant protein would occupy a large block of the shortlist and the ranking would report assembly depth rather than distinct candidates. The retained representative is the one sequence standing in for its whole family; the family itself may contain many near-duplicates.

Candidates within reach of a known plastizyme were also removed, so what remains cannot be a rediscovery of a database entry. That is a deliberate handicap: the easiest true positives to find are exactly the ones excluded.

Tier definitions

Tiers combine the two independent lines of evidence. The probe score is the classifier; the triad count is how many of the three catalytic residues the SAE latents place on a residue of the correct identity. A tier is not a confidence interval — it is a description of which evidence agreed.

Every tier requires probe ≥ —. Candidates below the threshold are not shortlisted at all. Within a tier, rows are ordered by probe score.

How to read this

  • These are computational nominations, not discoveries. Every row is a hypothesis about a sequence. Ranking them is the entire claim.
  • No experimental validation exists. Not one candidate has been expressed, purified or assayed against a polyester. A high score is not evidence of activity.
  • Precision here is far worse than the benchmark. The probe was trained and evaluated at 47.8% prevalence. The true rate of polyester-degrading enzymes in a metagenome is orders of magnitude lower, so at the same sensitivity the great majority of positives at this threshold will be false. The test-set precision does not transfer.
  • Rank matters more than the absolute score. The score is uncalibrated off-domain and the reference line in the header shows how far below trained positives these sit. Read the list as an ordering of what to try first, not as a probability.
  • BLAST was deliberately not run, so no homology comparison is shown. A BLAST column would make the shortlist collapse toward whatever is nearest a known plastizyme — the opposite of the question being asked. The screen is left as the probe's own opinion, audited by the SAE latents.
