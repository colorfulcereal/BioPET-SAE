## 1. Task definition (locked by the project lead, 2026-10-11)

- **Positive:** an enzyme experimentally reported to degrade a plastic whose backbone is joined by
  carboxylic ester bonds.
- **Hard negative:** an α/β-hydrolase-fold protein (InterPro IPR029058) with the same Ser–His–Asp
  triad, never reported to degrade a plastic.
- **Easy negatives:** wrong-fold ester hydrolases, tRNA-processing hydrolases, and naive controls.
- There is no fold clause in the positive definition: the fold of the positives is reported, not enforced.
- The PHA "generalisation to an unseen lineage" claim is **withdrawn** and must not be reintroduced.

**Wording rules.** Say "candidate nomination", never "discovery". Say the latents "fire at the predicted
catalytic machinery", never that they "prove" mechanism. Present the ablation as a planned causal test, not
a result. Prefer "polyester-degrading enzyme" or "polyester hydrolase" over the loose "plastizyme" in
formal text. Do **not** say other plastics are excluded because they are "hard to break".

**Snippet (definition).**
> We define a positive as an enzyme with an experimental report of degrading a plastic whose backbone is
> joined by carboxylic ester bonds. Negatives are graded by what they share with the positives: hard
> negatives carry the same α/β-hydrolase fold and Ser–His–Asp triad but have no report of plastic
> degradation; easy negatives differ in fold, in catalytic machinery, or in function altogether. A
> negative label therefore means "not reported", not "inactive".

> Sixty-four percent of positives fall in one homology component (a cutinase family), so every interval
> is bootstrapped over homology components.

### Why only ester-bonded plastics

- Esters are polar and hydrolysable. The α/β-hydrolase fold and its Ser–His–Asp triad evolved to cut them.
- Amide (polyamide) and urethane (polyurethane) bonds are also hydrolysable but need a different
  catalytic machine. PAZy lists 65 polyamide and 15 polyurethane degraders in our export.
- Polyolefins (PE, PP), polystyrene and PVC have all-carbon backbones with no hydrolysable bond. Reports
  for PE and PP involve oxidative enzymes (laccases, alkane hydroxylases) that modify the chain and do not
  recover monomers.
- Ester bonds are more labile than amide bonds because the amide nitrogen donates into the carbonyl
  (partial double-bond character), making the carbonyl less electrophilic. **[unverified: half-life figures
  not checked against a source; keep the statement qualitative]**
- **Caveat:** bond reactivity is not what limits polyester degradation. PET is hard because of
  crystallinity, rigidity and low water uptake; it degrades much faster near its glass transition
  (about 65–70 °C), which is why engineered enzymes such as LCC are thermostable. **[unverified: Tg
  figure from memory]**

## 3. The negatives (v1.1: 422)

| group | n | what it is | train / val / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| α/β hydrolase hard negatives | 50 | IPR029058, Ser–His–Asp triad, never reported on a plastic | 39 / 1 / 10 |
| Wrong-fold ester hydrolases | 36 | EC 3.1.1, serine nucleophile, other fold (GDSL-SGNH, patatin, β-lactamase) | 23 / 0 / 13 |
| tRNA-processing hydrolases | 99 | EC 3.1.1 by reaction (peptidyl-tRNA hydrolase, D-aminoacyl-tRNA deacylase); His only, no catalytic Ser | 56 / 40 / 3 |
| Naive controls | 237 | kinases, gyrases, oxygenases; not esterases | 166 / 35 / 36 |

- **Naive controls:** assumed not to cut a plastic. This is an inference from what they are, not a measurement.
- **Hard negatives:** same fold, same triad, same carboxylic-ester chemistry; no report of plastic activity.
  "Not reported" is not "inactive". Many have never been assayed on a plastic. Some may be weak degraders.
- **Evidence for that last point:** `P37957` (*B. subtilis* lipase) was a hard negative until it was found
  to be 94% identical to `LipBv`, which PAZy reports as a PET degrader. It was removed from v1.1.
  Consequence: the hard-cell AU-ROC is, if anything, a conservative estimate.
- **What the 'tier 3' label hides:** the legacy `3_fold_matched_esterase` tier has 184 members in v1.1, of
  which only 49 carry the α/β fold. Use `negative_group`, not `class_label`.

### What EC 3.1.1 does (and does not) mean
- EC 3.1.1 = carboxylic ester hydrolases (3 = hydrolases, 3.1 = acting on ester bonds). It is a **reaction
  class, not a fold**. PETase is EC 3.1.1.101.
- It says the enzyme cuts some carboxylic ester, usually a small molecule (p-nitrophenyl esters,
  triglycerides). It does not say the enzyme can attack an insoluble, hydrophobic polymer: that needs
  surface access, a binding cleft that fits a chain, and often thermostability.
- Search gotcha: `ec:3.1.1.*` is a loose prefix match (also catches 3.1.10–3.1.14). Use `ec:3.1.1.-`.
- UniProt counts (reviewed entries, queried 2026-11-25): IPR029058 3,156; with a curated active site
  2,454; IPR029058 and EC 3.1.1 1,172.

**Snippet.**
> A hard negative shares the positives' fold, catalytic triad and ester chemistry, so the model cannot
> separate them by recognising "a serine hydrolase". Because none has been assayed on a plastic, the label
> is a statement about the literature, not about the enzyme.

**v1 (811 rows):** train 303 / 285 (588), val 43 / 76 (119), test 42 / 62 (104); hard negatives 40 / 1 / 10.

- Positive components: train 36, val 21, test 20 (77 total). Largest component `BPS0190` holds 259 of the
  404 positives (64%), all in train; it was 256 of 388 (66%) in v1.
- The 50 hard negatives sit in 39 components (33 contain only negatives, mostly single proteins; 6 also
  contain positives and hold 10 hard negatives).
- Identity bound: max cross-split identity 0.3967 / 0.3800 / 0.3983 (train/val, train/test, val/test) in
  v1; the 16 v1.1 additions at 0.3967 at most. Identity is global Needleman–Wunsch (BLOSUM62, gap −11/−1,
  free end gaps), counted over the shorter sequence.
- Two numbers bound every claim: **50 hard negatives, one of them in validation,** and **64% of positives
  in one component.**

**Snippet.**
> Sequences are grouped into homology components at 40% identity and whole components are assigned to
> train, validation or test, so no train–test pair exceeds 40% identity. All confidence intervals resample
> positive and negative components separately, never proteins.

## 5. Results so far (read only on frozen v1; v1.1 shown for comparison)

Probe: ESM-2 650M, max-pooled layer 33, standardised on train only; SGD log-loss L2 (alpha = 1/(C·n)),
5000 epochs, class-balanced, seed 42; decision threshold = best-F1 on validation. Verified against stored
v1 scores (max |diff| = 0.0). BLASTp: database = train positives, score = −log10 best e-value, e-value
cut-off chosen on val by F1.

### v1 (test n = 104: 42 positives, 62 negatives)

|---|---|---|
| AU-ROC | 0.9681 | 0.9040 |
| AU-PRC | 0.9526 | 0.7552 |
| recall at 90% precision | 78.6% | 2.4% |
| α/β hard negatives (n=10), AU-ROC | 0.8167 [0.613, 0.962] | 0.4286 |
| wrong-fold (n=13) | 0.9890 | 0.9927 |
| naive (n=36) | 1.0000 | 0.996 |
| tRNA (n=3) | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

At the val-selected threshold: TP/FP/FN/TN 39/8/3/54 (probe), 40/8/2/54 (BLASTp).

### v1.1 (test n = 113: 51 positives, 62 negatives)

| | probe, sweep-selected (C=0.1, eta0=0.001) | probe, v1 settings (C=0.01, eta0=0.0001) | BLASTp |
|---|---|---|---|
| AU-ROC | 0.9643 [0.905, 0.998] | 0.9763 [0.929, 1.000] | 0.9102 [0.813, 0.990] |
| AU-PRC | 0.9544 [0.841, 0.998] | 0.9712 [0.887, 1.000] | 0.7964 [0.615, 0.988] |
| recall at 90% precision | 92.2% | 94.1% | 2.0% |
| α/β hard negatives (n=10), AU-ROC | 0.8216 [0.590, 0.976] | 0.8725 [0.688, 0.993] | 0.4627 |
| wrong-fold (n=13) | 0.9744 | 0.9864 | 0.9940 |

### How to read these (and what not to claim)
- **Test is spent.** It has been read several times; do not select on these numbers.
- **Differences between rows are noise.** Retraining on +7 positives / −1 hard negative moves AU-ROC by
  about ±0.005, and the hyperparameter cell by about ±0.01, both smaller than the hard-cell interval.
- **The sweep selection is not decisive.** Five cells lie within 0.025 val log-loss; small-C and
  large-eta0 cells changed a lot between v1 and v1.1, so SGD at 5000 epochs on about 600 rows is sensitive
  to data order.
- **BLASTp is below chance on the hard negatives** (AU-ROC 0.43 on v1, 0.46 on v1.1, n=10): a fold-matched
  non-degrader sits about as close to a trained positive as a true degrader does. On wrong-fold and naive
  negatives BLASTp matches or beats the probe, so the probe's advantage is concentrated in the hard cell.
  A paired bootstrap of the difference by group is **not yet run**.
- **The blended number is mostly free weight:** 39 of 62 v1 test negatives are separated perfectly.
- **The decision threshold was tuned where validation had 1 hard negative,** so precision does not transfer
  (v1: precision fell 0.148 from val to test while ranking metrics fell about 0.03–0.04).
- The 9 positives added to v1.1 test are all called by the probe and by BLASTp, but they share components
  with old test proteins and are not independent evidence. LipSh1–3 (755–766 aa, ~0.40 identity to test
  relatives) score only 0.14–0.21 against a threshold of 0.11.

**Snippet.**
> On sequences at most 40% identical to training data, the probe raised AU-PRC from 0.76 (BLASTp) to 0.95
> and recall at 90% precision from 2.4% to 78.6%. The gain is concentrated in the hard cell:
> BLASTp ranks the 10 fold-matched non-degraders no better than chance (AU-ROC 0.43), while the probe
> reaches 0.82 [0.61, 0.96] (component bootstrap, n=10, 7 negative components).

## 6. Explainability (v1; not yet re-run on v1.1)

- Seven SAE latents (InterPLM, ESM-2 650M layer 33): 9529 (Ser), 2661 (Asp), 7734 (His), 7942, 5201, 5255
  (pocket wall), 5271 (C-terminal second shell). Selected on train+val by Cliff's δ ≥ 0.9 and top-10
  stability ≥ 150 of 200 random-half draws, with no catalytic-residue supervision.
- Localisation to curated catalytic residues: **57 of 59 proteins (96.6%)**; 44 of 45 on held-out
  proteins; offsets Ser +1, Asp +3, His +1 (learned on 14). `BPS0016` is a subtilisin and its silence is a
  correct rejection, so 57 of 58 on fold-bearing proteins.
- Nulls: random position 3.2% (41/1,280); **all 10,240 latents through the identical pipeline: median 4.4%,
  none scoring higher** (25 tie latent 9529 on Ser, none passing selection); shuffled position 12.0 / 7.5 /
  6.5%.
- **What it does not show:** the latents fire on 100% of α/β hard negatives, 13.9% of wrong-fold
  hydrolases and 0.0% of tRNA hydrolases. They read out serine-hydrolase machinery. Against 41 fold-matched
  train+val negatives, δ is 0.506–0.716 per protein but 0.116 to −0.178 per component, with cluster-bootstrap
  intervals including zero. Verdict: **undetermined, not refuted.** Correlational only; no ablation done.
- None of the 16 v1.1 additions has a curated catalytic residue, so the 59-protein localisation set does
  not grow.
- **Planned causal test:** zero each selected feature and measure the drop in predicted probability, against
  a matched-control baseline. Not yet done.

## 8. Plan for v1.2 (the cleaner, more powerful dataset)

**Why.** The hard-negative cell is the only part of the benchmark that measures the claim, and it rests on
10 test proteins in 7 components (interval about [0.59, 0.98]). Halving the interval needs about 4× the
components. With 300–400 hard negatives in about 200 components and a 60/20/20 split, test would hold
roughly 60–80 (an estimate, not simulated).

**Sourcing (UniProt reviewed entries).** IPR029058 with a curated active site, never assayed on a plastic,
excluding cutinase and PETase families; take a spread across organisms and subfamilies, not the first 300.

**Risks to control.**
- Label noise: drop any candidate ≥ 90% identical to a positive (the `P37957`/`LipBv` case); flag ≥ 40%.
- No selection by probe score or by distance to the positives.
- Positives remain the other bottleneck: 64% in one component, and 20 positive components in test.

**Split design (one new split, decided after the fetch).** An earlier option was to only swap 9 hard-negative
components from train into val and 9 naive controls back (val 1 → 10 hard negatives, test untouched). That
only redistributes the 50 we have, so v1.2 should use the enlarged pool with one new split. The cost: the
test set changes, so v1 / v1.1 / v1.2 test numbers are no longer directly comparable.

**Planned outputs (for graphs).** Five training runs (seeds 42–46), mean ± SE across seeds, on the **test**
split: ROC table (common FPR grid), PR table with the val-optimal threshold marked (common recall grid),
probability histogram on a log10 scale (clip 1e-8), per-protein score table, summary metrics, per-negative-
group ROC/PR (hard negatives first), and the same for BLASTp. SE is the standard deviation across seeds
divided by √5 and captures training randomness only; component-bootstrap intervals are reported separately.
Validation curves, if shown, are labelled "tuning split, optimistic".

## 9. Methods facts worth keeping

- Embeddings: HuggingFace `EsmModel`, layers 1/9/18/24/30/33, mean and max pooled, CLS/EOS stripped,
  max length 1022. Re-embedding on Apple MPS reproduces stored embeddings exactly.
- Probe selection: C × eta0 grid of 5 × 4 (C 1e-4…1; eta0 1e-4…1e-1), chosen on val log-loss, because val
  AU-PRC saturates and would pick an unconverged corner. C = inverse L2 strength; eta0 = SGD step size.
- Thresholds: val best-F1 for both the probe and BLASTp (carried to test).
- Scripts (committed): `polyester_probe.py`, `polyester_sweep.py`, `polyester_blast.py`,
  `add_pazy_update.py`, `check_pazy_update_uniprot.py`, `build_release.py`, `build_handoff.py`.
- **Reproducibility gap:** the SAE selection, nulls and localisation were produced by uncommitted scripts;
  they must be rewritten and regression-tested on v1 before re-running.
- The metagenomic screen: 30,000 MGnify families; the logs record 165 shortlisted (probe ≥ 0.5; 40 with a
  complete triad). The "450 candidates at ≥ 0.9" figure supplied for the paper is **not in the logs
  [unverified]**; re-score before using it.

## 10. Errors caught during the project (honesty list for the methods or appendix)

- AU-ROC 0.9141 was attributed to the wrong-fold group; it belongs to wrong-fold + α/β combined (n=23). The
  wrong-fold group alone is 0.9890 (n=13).
- A per-protein bootstrap interval was reported on the hard cell; the component bootstrap is wider.
- `BPS0016` was described as a polyamidase; it is a PLA-degrading subtilisin.
- The PHA "unseen lineage" premise was asserted and never tested (14 of 36 share components with trained
  positives); the generalisation claim was withdrawn.
- A draft abstract used superseded numbers (172/176, 100 bootstraps) and merged two different nulls.
- The v1 BLASTp score is −log10 e-value; the bitscore does not reproduce the logged numbers.
